# Research questions and checkpoints

## Priority 1 — Determine what is actually deployed (documentary evidence)

Find official GLI/other certification identifiers, operator/regulator technical specifications, ΙΤΥΕ documentation, reseeding and generator–verifier protocol versions. Determine whether any 2026 generation design changed from the historical 2001–2004/2023 descriptions. **Do not infer the running algorithm from paper examples.**

## Priority 2 — Validate and expand draw-level dataset

Retrieve and independently verify an authoritative chronological archive, including exact draw IDs, local timestamps, number order if authentic, missing/cancelled draws and provenance. Compare overlapping third-party archives; publish checksums and cleaning decisions.

## Priority 3 — Preregister statistical tests

Use the full archive to test: global frequency and pair distributions, exact consecutive-overlap distribution, runs/autocorrelation, weekday/time-of-day effects, serial dependencies and fixed non-overlapping 80-draw windows. Correct for multiple comparisons, look-elsewhere and retrospective time-window choice. Preserve negative results.

## Priority 4 — Identify sampling implementation, if evidence permits

Review technical descriptions of the mapping from random bits to 5-of-35 labels. Falsify biased mapping hypotheses under a documented model; recognize that multiple uniform algorithms produce an identical law for public unordered tickets.

## Priority 5 — If a predictive claim is ever proposed

Declare model/seed/algorithm/configuration **before** future results exist, record immutable predictions and score on prospective unseen draws against exact random and appropriately search-corrected baselines. No such validated claim exists as of archive cut-off.

## Guardrails

- Never publish guesses as operator implementation facts.
- Treat absence of detectable bias as *failure to reject*, not proof of perfect randomness.
- Treat statistical deviation as *audit trigger*, not proof of interference.
- Do not optimize gambling strategy on backtests that used known draw outcomes.
